# Product Brief: Sanabel

## Executive Summary

Sanabel is a free online learning platform focused on software and coding education, where instructors upload courses and signed-in learners view them at no cost. A course is a collection of lessons, and a lesson can take any form — video, text, PDF, or otherwise. Learners can track their progress, earn certificates on completion, discuss lessons, rate courses, and search/browse by topic.

The name Sanabel ("ears of wheat," symbolizing growth and productivity) reflects the project's driving idea: learning to code should be free for everyone. This is a personal project for Ahmed, born from that conviction rather than a market gap or business opportunity — its first measure of success is simply existing and working, not revenue or scale.

## The Problem

Much of today's structured coding education sits behind paywalls or subscriptions — bootcamps charge thousands, and even platforms like Udemy or Coursera are free to browse but not free to learn from in depth. For someone who wants to teach coding without asking learners to pay, and for learners who cannot or do not want to pay, that gate is the whole obstacle. `[ASSUMPTION]` The specific learners this matters most for (e.g., self-taught developers with limited means, a particular community or region) haven't been defined further — worth sharpening if this grows beyond a personal project.

## Who This Serves

**Instructors** — people who want to share coding knowledge without a platform taking a cut or gating their content behind pricing tiers they don't control.

**Learners** — signed-in users who want structured software/coding courses (not just scattered videos) at no cost, with a sense of progress and community (discussion, ratings) along the way.

## The Solution

Sanabel gives instructors a place to upload coding courses built from lessons in whatever format suits the material, and gives any signed-in learner free access to all of it, with progress tracking, certificates, discussion, ratings, and topic search built in from the start. No paywall, no tiered access — sign in and learn.

## What Makes This Different

Being honest about this: nothing here is a technical or feature moat. Udemy, Coursera, and Khan Academy already offer free content in various forms, and platforms like freeCodeCamp already do free-forever coding education well. Sanabel's difference is its posture, not its mechanics — it exists purely to be free, permanently, without a freemium upsell or ad-supported catch. Donations may be added later as an optional way for learners to support instructors, but never as a gate on access. The "unfair advantage," such as it is, is that Ahmed controls the mission and doesn't need to monetize it. `[ASSUMPTION]` If this ever grows past a personal project, this section will need a harder answer to "why Sanabel over an existing free option like freeCodeCamp."

## Success Criteria

Starting bar, confirmed for a personal-project scope:
- Ahmed can create an instructor account, build a full course (multiple lessons, mixed formats), and publish it end-to-end.
- A learner can sign in, browse/search courses by topic, consume a full course, track their progress, and receive a certificate on completion.
- Learners can discuss and rate courses.
- The platform stays free with no monetization pressure introduced.

Revisit this section once the project has real usage — it should evolve from "does it work" to something more meaningful.

## Scope

**In (v1):**
- Instructor accounts with upload capability (create courses, add lessons)
- Lessons support video, text, PDF, and other formats within a course
- Learner accounts with sign-in required to view any course
- Progress tracking per learner, per course
- Certificates issued on course completion
- Comments/discussion on courses or lessons
- Ratings/reviews on courses
- Search and browse by topic/category (topics scoped to software/coding)
- Web application

**Out (for now):**
- Mobile app — `[ASSUMPTION]` treated as a possible later phase, not committed
- Donation-based support for instructors — confirmed as a possible later addition, explicitly optional and never a gate on access

One honest flag, not a re-litigation: bundling progress tracking, certificates, discussion, ratings, and search alongside the core upload/view loop is a substantial v1 for a solo build. Confirmed as intentional — noted here so it's a visible, deliberate choice rather than scope creep that snuck in unnoticed.
